# Fictional Worked Review: Shipment TEST-17

## Decision and scope

Review of the [fictional draft](02-draft-memo.md) against the five items in the [fictional packet](01-fictional-packet.md). The decision is whether to send a demand on April 9 stating unequivocally that notice was timely. April 9 is the internal decision date. ## Source trace and weighted findings

- **Decision-changing | High confidence in the source conflict; low confidence in the actual receipt date.** The draft equates check-in with receipt. F2, p. 2, row 11 supports April 4 check-in only; F2, p. 1, row 4 shows an April 2 dock delivery scan for the same shipment without a named recipient. Neither proves when Buyer took custody. Under F1, p. 1, § 4(a), day five is April 7 if receipt occurred April 2 and April 9 if receipt occurred April 4. F4, p. 1 shows the April 8 email included both fields required by § 4(b), but its timing depends on the receipt date.
- **Decision-changing | High confidence in the excerpt; low confidence in what the full portal record contains.** F3, p. 1, ticket 821 supports a ticket and issue description on April 7. The visible export lacks an identifier. It does not show whether the portal attached TEST-17 to the ticket when submitted. The draft’s assertion of compliant portal notice is not verified. If the then-existing record shows both required fields, April 7 falls within five days on either date in issue.
- **Material | High confidence in wording; legal effect unverified.** F5, p. 1 supports receipt of the April 8 message, not a statement about the deadline. The draft’s suggestion that Supplier dispensed with the deadline is unsupported by the reply. If that theory matters, counsel must review the complete record and applicable law before relying on it.
- **Cleanup | High confidence.** Replace broad references to “F2, p. 2” with “F2, p. 2, row 11” and use equivalent locators for the other records.

## Strongest competing interpretation

Supplier would argue the signed excerpt runs from receipt, not from Buyer’s inventory entry. The same-shipment delivery scan is earlier, so the April 8 email was one day late if the dock delivery put the goods in Buyer’s custody. The April 7 ticket is within five days on that reading, but the only ticket record supplied does not display the identifier required for either notice route. This interpretation is grounded in the record even though custody and the full ticket are both unresolved.

## Recommendation and next steps

**Hold the categorical demand.** Do not represent the notice as conclusively timely on this record. Reconsider if the complete contemporaneous portal record establishes both required fields on April 7, or if reliable delivery evidence shows April 4 receipt and counsel confirms the relevant contract terms and applicable law. If April 2 receipt is confirmed and the authoritative portal record lacks a shipment identifier, revise the advice around the materially weaker notice position.

1. **Matter counsel, first on April 9:** Confirm the complete agreement, including notice language, date/time conventions, amendments, and any independent deadline. If something expires before review is complete, decide immediately how to preserve the client’s position without making the disputed assertion.
2. **Case team, same day if available:** Ask the authorized internal custodian for the original proof of delivery and dock receiving records for TEST-17. Compare the records for actual handoff, not just scanner or inventory timestamps.
3. **Case team, in parallel:** Obtain from authorized sources the complete historical ticket and linked-object fields as of submission. Distinguish a link present at creation from a later edit.
4. **Matter counsel, after reconciliation:** Select the applicable timetable or explain why the receipt date remains disputed. If a governing-law or waiver argument is necessary, verify it against primary authority before stating a legal conclusion.
