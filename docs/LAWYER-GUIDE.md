# Lawyer Guide: Review a Memo Before Relying on It

## What to hand the reviewer

Provide the draft to be reviewed, the question it is supposed to answer, and the sources needed to verify its consequential claims. Identify the expected reader, applicable forum if known, and the decision date. State whether research is permitted and whether any sources are known to be incomplete. If the decision date is an internal target, do not label it a filing or notice deadline.

You do not need to send the whole file. Start with the draft, the few documents it cites, and any record that seems to tell a different story. For an AI-assisted review, confirm that the destination, account, retention terms, and access permissions are suitable before sharing confidential materials. Use the included fictional packet if you are just evaluating the workflow. The workflow itself does not confer confidentiality or privilege.

## Start with a narrow instruction

Copy [the skill instructions](../SKILL.md) into an approved assistant or use the [worksheet](REVIEW-WORKSHEET.md) manually. Give the reviewer something like this:

> Review this draft; do not redraft it. The decision is whether to send the proposed demand by Thursday. Separate supported facts from inferences. Pin every decision-changing finding to an exact source locator. Argue the strongest alternative fairly, then give one recommendation, its reversal trigger, and a numbered next-step list. If you cannot verify something, say so. Do not contact anyone or take action.

Replace “Thursday” with a date and time zone when timing matters. If only an excerpt or export is available, identify it as such. Ask the reviewer to note the scope of the search and any missing documents rather than inferring that an absent result means the record does not exist.

## Use a three-pass review

1. **Check the pivotal facts.** Read the source passages yourself. Confirm date sequence, actor, capacity, and the distinction between a record and an inference. Ask what would disprove the fact. Document a conflicting source even if the memo can ultimately reconcile it.
2. **Check the legal hinge.** If the recommendation turns on a legal proposition, find and read primary authorities. Confirm version, jurisdiction, procedural posture, controlling status, and contrary treatment. Separate the reviewer's ability to retrieve a text from your determination that the cited authority supports this use. If access is limited, the deliverable is a verification list, not a verified legal conclusion.
3. **Check the action.** Demand one next move and ask why it beats the nearest alternative today. Confirm who owns each follow-up, when it should be done, and what changes the recommendation. If any deadline is unconfirmed, prioritize checking it independently; an internal review hold should not silently let time run.

## Accept or return the review

Accept it when every pivotal assertion has an exact locator or a conspicuous gap; the strongest adverse reading is given a fair statement; risk weight is distinct from confidence; and the next recommendation makes sense even if some inferences later change. Return the review if it substitutes polished prose for source checks, assigns unsupported percentages, or claims an authority is good law without describing what was actually checked.

Keep the original draft. Park the review with the underlying source packet and, after fixing the decision-changing items, recheck only the affected claims and conclusion unless the scope has changed. If the review recommends revising the memo, do that as a separate step so the reviewer’s findings are not lost in a redline. This kit is for licensed attorneys, not a substitute for advice, professional judgment, or applicable local rules.
